**INSTRUCTIONS: PART 2 - MY PLAN**
Based on the transcript, select the SINGLE most relevant strategy from the "Knowledge Base" below to act as the parent's active protocol. 

*Strategy Knowledge Base (Setup for Success):*
1. **Set a clear screen time routine:** Create consistent routines so the child knows when screen time happens and ends. Predictability helps toddlers feel safe and reduces pushback.
2. **Plan the transition before you turn it off (High Priority):** Decide what comes next and tell the child ahead of time. Use a positive next activity (blocks, coloring, snack time, outside).
3. **Choose a natural stopping point (High Priority):** Stop at the end of an episode, level, or song instead of turning it off mid-way. Makes the ending feel fair.
4. **Give simple countdown warnings (High Priority):** Short warnings ("5 minutes left", "2 minutes left", "Last one, then we stop") help the child adjust.
5. **Use a timer to make it neutral (High Priority):** A visual timer shifts the boundary to the routine, becoming the signal instead of the parent.
6. **Give effective command:** Direct, Positively Stated, Specific, One at a time, Developmentally appropriate, Normal tone. Explain before or after obeying. (e.g., "Turn off the TV." Using "please" is okay).
7. **Effective follow through:** 
   - *Praise:* If obeyed within 5 seconds, give labeled praise.
   - *The Choice Flow:* If ignored, offer a logical "This or That" choice, age-timed. (e.g., "You have two choices, either you turn off the TV, or I turn off for you.")
   - *Expected Behavior vs Nagging:* If a privilege is removed, make a brief, matter-of-fact statement ("You didn't do what I told you, so I turned it off for you"). Flag "Nagging" if the parent repeatedly explains rules or pleads while the child refuses.
8. **Co-regulate first, then teach:** If upset, start with calm support. Name the feeling, stay close, hold the limit. 
9. **Redirecting for bridging activity & Modeling (High Priority):** Start playing the bridging activity and describe your actions. Ignore the child until they join you. Once they join, offer labeled praise.
10. **Stay consistent even if there is a protest (High Priority):** Do not give extra screen time for tantrums. Keep the limit, stay calm, and repeat the routine.
*Progression Rules:*
- If all steps above fail, introduce special play time ("emotional massage").
- Once special play time mastery/score reaches 80, suggest using it as a bridging activity.
- Following that, offer the parent the option to practice discipline.
